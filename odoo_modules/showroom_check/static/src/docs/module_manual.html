What Showroom Check does

It proves two things at once: that somebody actually visited the room, and that the room still looks the way it is meant to look. You photograph the room once, from one spot, looking each of four ways, with everything where it belongs. Every round after that is compared against those photographs, and anything that no longer matches is flagged.

Before you start. The camera only works on a page whose address begins with https. That is a browser rule, not a setting in here, and nothing on the dashboard will work until your server is reached over https. Setting up needs the Manager role; day-to-day recording needs only User.

How the check works

Every office has exactly four views, fixed forever: Front, Right, Back and Left. You give each one a name in your own words — name it after what is in it rather than which way it faces, because that name is what people are shown when they are asked to photograph it.

  1. Originals — four photographs, taken once.
  2. Window opens — the round's time arrives.
  3. Capture — staff record the round.
  4. Scored — compared and flagged.

A view with no original is invisible to the whole feature: it is not asked for, not stored and not scored. Start with one or two views you care about; you do not have to set up all four on day one.

Part 1 — Setting up

Done once, by a manager.

Give people a role

Under Settings → Users, pick the person and choose under Showroom Check:

  • No access — cannot open Showroom Check at all.
  • User — opens the dashboard and captures a round while its window is open. Sees Check and Help, and nothing else.
  • Manager — everything above, plus the settings, the rounds, the originals, the reports, and deleting a recording.

History and Comparisons are a manager's screens. A User still sees on the dashboard that a colleague's round was done, but cannot open that colleague's video or photographs. Only a Manager can delete a recording, which is the only way to record the same round twice in one day.

Set the daily rounds

Configuration → Settings → Daily Rounds. Set the Office Timezone first, then add one line per visit. Three rules the system will not let you break:

  • A round cannot run past midnight. For 22:00–02:00, add two rounds.
  • Two rounds cannot overlap on the same day — the dashboard would not know which one is being recorded.
  • A round with no day ticked is refused; it would never open.

Every time you type is read in the Office Timezone, for everybody — not in each person's own profile timezone. Use the Instructions field for anything needed at that hour ("include the pantry and both washrooms"); it is shown on the dashboard card while the window is open, and again in the camera window.

Then

  • Photograph the originals — once, from the spot every round will be walked from.
  • Choose what a round records — video, photographs, or both.
  • Say who can record.
  • Decide how long to keep recordings.
  • Turn on AI review — optional, and advisory only.

Part 2 — Everyday use

Staff open the dashboard and capture a round while its window is open; the server scores it straight away. Afterwards, History lists what was recorded and the comparison shows how it scored.

Reading the numbers

  • Match (arrangement) — has anything moved? The score of the worst view, not the average, because the worst view is the entire reason anybody is looking.
  • Average Match — the mean across the views. Too smooth to warn on, but the right number to watch over weeks.
  • Similar — is this even the same view? How much of the same thing is in both pictures.
  • Matching features — press it to see the two pictures side by side with a line drawn between every patch found in both.

Similar and Match answer different questions and are easy to confuse. A filthy room photographed from exactly the right spot is 100% similar and 0% matched — and that pair of numbers is the whole point. Similar tells you the photograph is of the right thing; Match tells you whether anything in it has moved.

The verdicts

  • As expected — at or above Warn Below. Nothing to do.
  • Worth a look — between the two thresholds. Open the round and compare the pictures yourself.
  • Something has changed — below Alert Below. Look at the worst view and find out why.
  • Could not line up — the right view, but the two pictures could not be aligned; usually photographed from a very different spot.
  • Not compared — never compared, or the comparison failed. Press Compare again.

Both thresholds are applied when the page is drawn, never stored. Change them and the whole history is re-graded instantly.

Part 3 — Keeping in order

Reading the reports, fixing a view that will not line up, watching storage, and getting steadier scores.

Quick tips

  • The whole schedule runs on the Office Timezone, not on each person's own.
  • A view with no original is never asked for and never scored.
  • A good match is 70–80, not 100. Light changes, and people do not stand in the same place twice.
  • Only a manager deleting a recording re-opens a round. It cannot be archived.
  • No sound is recorded — consider telling the people being filmed; in many places that is a legal requirement.
  • Deleting a recording does not free disk space at once; the file goes in a later clean-up.
  • The AI is advisory. Nothing is ever rejected automatically.
  • A round walked as a video is scored on frames cut out of it. Read those a little more gently than an aimed photograph.

If something goes wrong

  • "Recording needs a secure (https) connection" — the page has to be served over https, or the browser blocks the camera. Nothing is wrong with the camera.
  • "The camera is already being used by another program" — close Teams, Zoom or any other video app completely, then Try again.
  • "Camera access was blocked" — click the camera icon in the address bar, choose Allow, then try again.
  • The button is greyed out during the window — either the round is already recorded today, or you are not on the list under Who Can Record. The card says which.
